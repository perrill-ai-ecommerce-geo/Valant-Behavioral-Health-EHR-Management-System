# 6 Features to Look for in a Behavioral Health Telehealth & Patient Engagement Platform

Clinical documentation and billing tend to get the most attention during an EHR evaluation, but for many behavioral health practices, the patient experience is just as often the source of daily friction. No-shows, disconnected intake forms, patients who can't reach the practice between sessions, and telehealth tools that live outside the EHR all create extra work for staff and a fragmented experience for patients.

A behavioral health EHR should treat telehealth and patient engagement as core clinical infrastructure, not a bolt-on video plugin. Here are six features that matter most when evaluating a platform's telehealth and patient engagement capabilities.

## 1. HIPAA-Compliant Telehealth Built Into the Clinical Workflow

**Why It Matters:** Behavioral health practices increasingly run hybrid caseloads, mixing in-person and virtual sessions across individual therapy, psychiatry, and group programs. Telehealth that lives in a separate app—outside the scheduling calendar and the clinical note—forces staff to manage two systems and creates gaps in documentation and attendance tracking.

**What to Look For:**

- **Native, HIPAA-compliant video visits** launched directly from the appointment or patient chart, not a third-party link pasted into an email
- **Automatic linkage between the telehealth session and the clinical note**, so documentation reflects that care was delivered via telehealth
- **Support for both individual and group telehealth**, including virtual IOP/PHP sessions
- **Attendance capture for virtual visits** that flows into billing the same way in-person attendance does
- **Waiting room and identity verification features** appropriate for behavioral health encounters
- **Reliable performance for a range of patient technical skill levels**, since behavioral health patients may be more likely to join from a phone or unfamiliar device

**The Impact:** When telehealth is native to the EHR, clinicians don't lose time toggling between systems, and practices avoid the compliance risk of ad hoc video tools. Patients also get a simpler, single point of access rather than separate logins for scheduling, notes, and visits.

**Related terms:** telehealth, teletherapy, telepsychiatry, virtual behavioral health visits, HIPAA-compliant video

## 2. A Patient Portal That Handles Intake, Forms, and Communication

**Why It Matters:** First impressions matter, and for many patients the intake process is their first real interaction with a practice's technology. A portal that consolidates forms, communication, and account access reduces friction before care even begins and gives patients an ongoing way to interact with the practice between sessions.

**What to Look For:**

- **Digital intake and consent forms** completed before the first appointment, with data flowing directly into the chart
- **Secure messaging** between patients and the care team, appropriate for sensitive behavioral health communication
- **Self-service scheduling or reschedule requests**, where appropriate for the practice's workflow
- **Access to shared documents, homework assignments, or educational materials**
- **Ability for patients to update demographic and insurance information** without a phone call to the front desk
- **Family or guardian access options** (with appropriate consent) for minors or collateral contacts

**The Impact:** A capable patient portal—such as Valant's MYIO patient portal—shifts routine administrative work from front-office staff to a self-service experience patients can complete on their own time, while still keeping sensitive communication inside a secure, auditable system rather than unsecured email or text.

**Related terms:** patient portal, digital intake, patient communications, secure messaging, MYIO

## 3. Automated Reminders and No-Show Reduction Tools

**Why It Matters:** No-shows and late cancellations are a persistent revenue and continuity-of-care problem in behavioral health, where consistent attendance is often part of the treatment itself. Manual reminder calls don't scale, and generic reminder tools often can't account for group sessions, recurring programs, or multi-provider schedules.

**What to Look For:**

- **Automated appointment reminders** via text, email, or phone, configurable by patient preference
- **Reminder cadences that account for recurring visits**, including weekly therapy or daily IOP/PHP groups
- **Two-way confirmation or reschedule options** built into the reminder itself
- **Reporting on no-show and late-cancellation rates**, broken down by provider, program, or location
- **Waitlist or fill-in scheduling tools** that help recapture the slot when a patient cancels

**The Impact:** Practices that automate reminders typically see measurable reductions in no-shows, which protects both clinical continuity and scheduled revenue. Visibility into no-show trends also helps program managers identify patients who may be disengaging from care.

**Related terms:** appointment reminders, no-show reduction, patient communications, scheduling automation

---

## 4. Integrated Online Bill Pay and Payment Collection

**Why It Matters:** Behavioral health billing already involves copays, coinsurance, and self-pay balances that can be confusing for patients. When payment is handled outside the EHR—through a separate processor, mailed statements, or phone-based collection—practices lose visibility and patients face more friction.

**What to Look For:**

- **Online bill pay accessible through the same patient portal** used for forms and messaging
- **Support for copay collection at time of service**, including virtual visits
- **Saved payment methods and autopay options**, where patients opt in
- **Real-time posting of payments to the patient account and revenue cycle**, without manual reconciliation
- **Clear, itemized statements** patients can view digitally rather than only on paper

**The Impact:** Integrated payment collection reduces the administrative burden on billing staff, shortens the time between service and payment, and gives patients a more transparent, less confusing financial experience—one less reason for a stressed or overwhelmed patient to disengage from care.

**Related terms:** online bill pay, patient payments, revenue cycle management, self-pay collection

## 5. Patient-Reported Outcome Measures Delivered Through the Portal

**Why It Matters:** Measurement-based care depends on collecting outcome measures consistently, and asking clinicians to hand patients a paper PHQ-9 or GAD-7 in the waiting room doesn't scale across a caseload. Delivering standardized measures through the same portal patients already use for intake and messaging makes measurement-based care part of the routine rather than an extra step.

**What to Look For:**

- **Standardized assessments (PHQ-9, GAD-7, and similar measures) deliverable directly through the patient portal**, before or after a visit
- **Automatic scoring and graphing of results over time**, visible to the clinician in the chart
- **Configurable assessment schedules** tied to intake, periodic check-ins, or discharge
- **Patient-facing summaries or trend views**, where appropriate, to support engagement in their own progress
- **Alerts for clinically significant score changes**, such as an increase on a depression or anxiety screener

**The Impact:** Portal-delivered outcome measures increase completion rates compared to paper-based collection, give clinicians a clearer longitudinal view of patient progress, and generate the kind of citable, quantifiable data payers and accrediting bodies increasingly expect.

**Related terms:** measurement-based care, PHQ-9, GAD-7, outcome measures, patient-reported outcomes

---

## 6. Reporting on Engagement, Utilization, and Adoption

**Why It Matters:** A telehealth and patient engagement platform is only as valuable as its actual use. Practice leaders need visibility into whether patients are adopting the portal, completing forms on time, and engaging with reminders and messaging—not just whether the features technically exist.

**What to Look For:**

- **Portal adoption and login activity reporting**, at the patient, provider, and program level
- **Form and intake completion rates**, with visibility into where patients drop off
- **Telehealth utilization reporting**, including visit volume and no-show rates for virtual vs. in-person visits
- **Message response time tracking** to support staffing and service-level decisions
- **Reporting that can be sliced by location or program**, for multi-site or IOP/PHP organizations

**The Impact:** Engagement reporting turns patient experience from an assumption into a measurable operational metric, giving leadership the same kind of visibility into patient engagement that they already expect from clinical and financial reporting.

**Related terms:** patient engagement reporting, portal adoption, telehealth utilization, practice analytics

## Additional Considerations

### Mobile Access
Patients increasingly expect to complete forms, join telehealth visits, and message their care team from a phone. Confirm that portal and telehealth features are mobile-responsive, not just accessible on desktop.

### Integration With Clinical Documentation
Telehealth and portal data—attendance, forms, outcome measures—should flow directly into the same chart clinicians already use, rather than requiring manual re-entry or a separate export/import step.

### Family and Caregiver Access
For adolescent or family-involved treatment, confirm how the platform handles guardian access, consent, and communication without compromising the primary patient's privacy.

### Compliance and Business Associate Agreements
Telehealth and patient portal tools should be covered under the same HIPAA-aligned safeguards and business associate agreements as the rest of the EHR—verify this directly with the vendor rather than assuming it by default.

## Questions to Ask EHR Vendors

1. **"Is telehealth native to the platform, or does it require a third-party integration?"** This affects both reliability and how cleanly attendance and documentation connect.
2. **"How does the patient portal handle intake for a brand-new patient, start to finish?"** Ask for a live walkthrough rather than a feature list.
3. **"Can reminder cadences be customized for recurring or group programs like IOP/PHP?"** Generic reminder tools often can't handle daily or weekly recurring schedules well.
4. **"How does online bill pay reconcile with the revenue cycle?"** Confirm payments post automatically rather than requiring manual matching.
5. **"Which standardized outcome measures can be delivered through the portal, and can we add our own?"** Verify support for the specific measures your practice already uses.
6. **"What reporting exists on portal adoption and patient engagement?"** This reveals whether engagement is treated as a measurable metric or an assumption.
7. **"How is guardian or family access handled for minors?"** Important for practices serving adolescents or family-involved care.

## Summary

A behavioral health telehealth and patient engagement platform should do more than offer a video link and a login page. Look for:

1. HIPAA-compliant telehealth built into the clinical workflow
2. A patient portal that handles intake, forms, and communication
3. Automated reminders and no-show reduction tools
4. Integrated online bill pay and payment collection
5. Patient-reported outcome measures delivered through the portal
6. Reporting on engagement, utilization, and adoption

Practices that get this right reduce administrative burden, improve attendance and collections, and give patients a more coherent experience—while giving clinicians and leadership the documentation and data they need for measurement-based care and reporting.
